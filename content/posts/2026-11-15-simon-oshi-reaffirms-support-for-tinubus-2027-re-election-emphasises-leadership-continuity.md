Simon Oshi Reaffirms Support for Tinubu’s 2027 Re-election, Emphasizes Leadership Continuity

Former Special Adviser and Commissioner, Hon. Simon Oshi, has reiterated his support for President Bola Tinubu’s second-term ambition, maintaining that Nigeria’s complex political and economic challenges require sustained leadership and policy continuity.

Oshi believes that President Tinubu should be allowed to complete the constitutionally permitted eight-year tenure, arguing that the country’s longstanding development challenges cannot be comprehensively addressed within a single four-year presidential term.

Speaking with journalists on November 9, 2026, Oshi maintained that Nigeria’s political environment presents several challenges to the implementation of major government policies.

He explained that the competing interests of political parties, state governors, lawmakers and other stakeholders often influence the pace of decision-making and the execution of national development initiatives.

According to him, these realities make it necessary for governments to approach national transformation with patience, strategic planning and sustained commitment.

Understanding the Challenges of Governance

Oshi acknowledged that Nigeria has numerous economic and structural challenges requiring deliberate government intervention.

However, he argued that addressing these issues involves navigating a complex political and institutional framework that makes immediate transformation difficult.

He maintained that even a president with executive authority must work within constitutional boundaries and engage relevant institutions when pursuing significant policy changes.

In his remarks, he stressed that the different interests within Nigeria’s political system require careful consideration and negotiation before major decisions can be fully implemented.

He therefore believes that a longer period of consistent leadership could provide an opportunity to pursue and consolidate development policies.

Why Oshi Advocates a Second Term for Tinubu

Explaining his position on the 2027 presidential election, Oshi maintained that President Tinubu deserves another term to continue the policy direction of his administration.

He argued that changing leadership before major reforms have had sufficient time to mature could create challenges for the continuity of national developmental progress. 

Oshi also expressed the view that the administration’s policies are gradually influencing the country’s circumstances, making sustained implementation an important consideration.

His position reflects his broader argument that meaningful national development requires more than short-term expectations.

The Southeast and Nigeria’s Political Future

Oshi’s comments also addressed the Southeast’s aspiration to produce Nigeria’s next president.

While acknowledging that the region has competent individuals capable of occupying the highest political office, he maintained that its immediate presidential ambition should be reconsidered in the context of the 2027 election.

He urged political leaders and presidential aspirants from the region to support Tinubu’s second-term ambition rather than pursue a Southeast presidential candidate in the immediate electoral cycle.

He has also identified Abia State Governor, Alex Otti, as a potential presidential contender from the Southeast in 2031.

This position highlights his view that the region’s political aspirations can remain part of the national conversation while considering the timing of future electoral opportunities.

Looking Ahead

As Nigeria prepares for the 2027 general elections, discussions surrounding leadership continuity, regional representation and national development remain important aspects of the country’s political discourse.

Oshi’s position centers on the belief that sustained governance, institutional cooperation and policy consistency are necessary considerations in addressing Nigeria’s longstanding challenges.

He maintains that the country needs a development approach that recognizes the complexities of governance while allowing sufficient time for policies and reforms to be implemented.
