Good Things Take Time: Simon Oshi Urges Nigerians to Exercise Patience with Tinubu’s Administration

Hon. Simon Oshi has called on Nigerians to exercise patience with President Bola Tinubu’s administration, arguing that the country’s economic and structural challenges require time, consistent policies and sustained leadership to address.

Oshi, who supports President Tinubu’s re-election bid in 2027, believes the President should be given the opportunity to complete his constitutionally permitted eight-year tenure, maintaining that meaningful national transformation cannot be achieved within a single four-year term.

Speaking with journalists, Oshi acknowledged the difficulties confronting Nigerians but maintained that the administration’s reform agenda requires sufficient time to produce its intended outcomes.

According to him, Nigeria’s complex political structure, characterised by competing interests among federal and state governments, lawmakers and political parties, makes the implementation of far-reaching reforms a demanding process.

He argued that national challenges cannot be resolved overnight, even with a government committed to introducing significant policy changes.

Policy Continuity and National Development

Oshi maintained that continuity in leadership provides an opportunity for governments to consolidate existing policies, address implementation challenges and pursue long-term development objectives.

He referenced the major economic reforms introduced by the Tinubu administration, including the removal of the petroleum subsidy and changes to the foreign exchange framework, noting that significant policy adjustments require time and consistent implementation.

While acknowledging the economic pressures experienced by Nigerians, he argued that the administration’s initiatives should be assessed within the broader context of the structural challenges they seek to address.

He also highlighted the importance of legislative cooperation, explaining that the President must work within Nigeria’s constitutional framework and secure the necessary institutional support to advance major policy decisions.

Drawing Lessons from the Biblical Story of Moses

To further illustrate his position, Oshi referred to the biblical account of Moses leading the Israelites out of Egypt.

He explained that the journey required persistence, determination and the ability to overcome significant obstacles before the desired outcome could be achieved.

Drawing a parallel with Nigeria’s development journey, he argued that meaningful national transformation similarly requires patience and sustained commitment.

He also pointed to the legislative processes involved in governance, noting that presidential authority operates within constitutional and institutional boundaries.

Leadership, Performance and Continuity

Oshi also commended Abia State Governor Alex Otti, whom he considers a potential Southeast presidential contender in 2031, highlighting leadership, hard work and continuity as important considerations in governance.

He maintained that Nigerians should evaluate political officeholders based on their performance, visible achievements and contributions to society rather than relying exclusively on political party affiliations.

According to him, responsible leadership requires a commitment to delivering measurable results and pursuing policies that contribute to national development.

Looking Towards Nigeria’s Future

As discussions surrounding the country’s political and economic future continue, Oshi has reiterated his belief that sustained leadership and policy consistency are important elements of national development.

He urged Nigerians to consider the complexities of governance and the time required to implement structural reforms, maintaining that long-term progress demands patience, institutional cooperation and commitment.

For Oshi, the message is straightforward: meaningful national transformation requires more than immediate expectations; it requires sustained effort, continuity and a collective commitment to building a better Nigeria.
