As Nigeria gradually approaches the 2027 general elections, Enugu State politician and philanthropist Simon Oshi has shared his perspective on the country’s political future, arguing that Nigeria’s complex challenges cannot be resolved within a single four-year presidential administration.

Speaking with journalists, Oshi maintained that the country’s development challenges are deeply rooted and require sustained leadership, institutional cooperation and a long-term approach to governance.

According to him, no presidential candidate should be expected to transform Nigeria completely within four years, noting that the country’s political structure, competing interests and constitutional framework present significant considerations for any administration.

The Challenge of National Transformation

Oshi explained that governing Nigeria requires navigating several institutional and political interests, including the National Assembly, state governments and different political parties.

He argued that meaningful national transformation requires patience, consistency and cooperation among the various arms and levels of government.

“You can’t change Nigeria for good in four years; it is not possible, even in eight years, except you are a magician,” he said.

He further questioned the practicality of promises to transform the country within a single presidential term, stressing that national development is a gradual process that requires continuity.

His Position on the 2027 Presidential Election

Addressing the emerging political conversations surrounding the 2027 presidential election, Oshi expressed support for President Bola Tinubu’s second-term ambition.

He argued that the President should be given the opportunity to continue his administration’s policies and development agenda, maintaining that sustained leadership is important in addressing Nigeria’s longstanding challenges.

Oshi also discussed the political ambitions of other prominent presidential figures, including former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi and former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar.

He questioned the prospects of a one-term administration achieving the scale of transformation Nigeria requires, arguing that the country’s challenges demand more than short-term political commitments.

Constitutional Institutions and Governance

Oshi also highlighted the importance of constitutional institutions in the administration of the country.

He maintained that a Nigerian president cannot independently reshape the country’s governing framework or implement every policy without engaging the National Assembly and other relevant institutions.

According to him, cooperation between the executive and legislative arms of government remains essential to achieving meaningful policy outcomes.

A Call for Patience and Continuity

Reiterating his position, Oshi emphasized that Nigeria’s development journey requires deliberate planning, institutional collaboration and sustained commitment.

His comments add to the growing national conversation surrounding leadership, political continuity and the expectations Nigerians should have of those seeking the country’s highest political office ahead of the 2027 general elections.
